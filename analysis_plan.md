# Market-condition analysis specification

Written September 29, 2026, before fitting the linked models. This is an analysis decision log, not external preregistration. The earlier NHIS employment-status analysis did not address the market-condition question and will be archived.

Question: Among working-age adults who are out of work, is poorer mental health associated with a state labor market in which fewer hires occur, conditional on the unemployment rate? Does a measure of competition for vacancies give a consistent signal?

Public sources: BRFSS annual respondent files 2013–2025 (the EMPLOY1 series, common current employment question); BLS state JOLTS and LAUS. US states and DC, age 18–64. Include EMPLOY1=3 or 4 (out of work >=1 year or <1 year). These categories do NOT verify active job search. Employed adults (EMPLOY1=1/2) form a separate contextual analysis. Do not call them a randomized control group. Exclude invalid outcome, demographic covariates, or weight; show flow by year and missing states. Do not select years based on results.

Primary outcome: >=14 of the past 30 days on which mental health was not good (MENTHLTH 14–30); 88 means zero; 77/99/blank are missing. Secondary outcome: number of poor mental health days (0–30). This is self-reported distress, not psychiatric diagnosis.

Primary exposure: calendar-year mean of the 12 seasonally adjusted state monthly hires rates. Report per one percentage-point lower rate (hires per 100 payroll employees); not a personal job-finding probability. Primary model includes same-year mean state unemployment rate. Secondary separate models: log2(unemployed persons per job opening), and job-opening rate. Do not combine a ratio with its unemployment numerator as though independent exposures. All monthly series must have 12 observations. Annual exposure deliberately measures the broader survey-year environment, not a precisely ordered pre-interview treatment.

Regression: survey-weighted linear probability model with state fixed effects, year fixed effects, age groups (18–24, then five-year groups to 60–64), sex as released, education (<high school/high school/some college/college), and the common five-level race/ethnicity variable. No income, disability, or duration control in primary model: these may be consequences or selection mechanisms. Estimate absolute percentage-point differences. Cluster robust errors by state (51 clusters), CR1 correction and t distribution with G-1 degrees of freedom. State clustering covers dependence within state over time and across respondents; this is not a full BRFSS design-based variance or propagated uncertainty in model-assisted JOLTS estimates. Inference remains observational and exploratory.

Specified checks: (1) primary hiring model without unemployment adjustment; (2) prior-calendar-year hiring/unemployment; (3) exclude 2020–2021; (4) state-specific linear trends; (5) equal total weight per state-year; (6) restrict to states with all years; (7) recent and long-duration out-of-work strata; (8) employed comparison, separately estimated; (9) continuous mental-health-days outcome. Publish every specified result, including nulls and reversals. Leave-one-state-out influence and residual exposure variation will be inspected. Avoid claim of robustness if direction or precision is sensitive.

Health selection into out-of-work status, changing composition, remote/interstate searching, time-varying state confounding, annual exposure timing, survey nonresponse, changing state availability, and state JOLTS modeling are central limitations. A causal claim about platforms, ghost jobs, psychiatric onset, or the effect of altering hiring rates is not identified. No SEM: mediators and temporal ordering are not observed.

Figure plan: one simple coefficient plot for primary and explicitly specified alternatives, clear zero line and 95% CIs; optional descriptive annual mental-health and market panels with separate axes, never dual axes. No synthetic recession score. Claims must follow the estimates, not an intended thesis.

Source clarification before fitting: unemployment adjustment uses the BLS published LAUS annual-average rate (M13, unadjusted); JOLTS exposures use arithmetic means of the 12 seasonally adjusted monthly rates/ratios. _RACEGR4 in 2022 has the same five output categories as _RACEGR3 in other years. BRFSS survey year, not the occasional following-January interview year, defines the linkage.

Pre-fit completeness decision: October 2025 LAUS observations were not collected during the federal shutdown; BLS flags the published 2025 annual averages G, and the corresponding October unemployed/opening ratio is absent. Therefore the primary analysis uses complete calendar years 2013–2024. A separately labeled 2013–2025 hiring extension will use the published 11-month unemployment annual input. No ratio interpolation or 11-month ratio masquerading as a complete year is allowed. This decision was made before any linked regression results were produced. Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/laus_01072026.htm

Post-fit documentation, same date: the clearest explicit BLS statement about the 11-month annual averages was subsequently verified in the April 8 release, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/laus_04082026.htm; the manuscript cites that notice. This source clarification does not change the pre-fit completeness decision. After observing the linear model's small fraction of out-of-range fitted probabilities, a logistic model with the same regressors, state-clustered uncertainty and a delta-method average marginal association was added as a **post hoc model-form check**. It is labeled accordingly in the manuscript and is not presented as prespecified.
