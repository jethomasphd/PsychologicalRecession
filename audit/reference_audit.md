# Reference audit

Verification date: 2026-09-29. The revised manuscript contains 15 references: 12 journal articles, two official NHIS documentation records, and the reproducibility repository. Every retained source has a stated claim and limit. DOI records establish identity, not claim support. Review depth is recorded honestly; this is not a full-text systematic review or a formal retraction-database audit.

## Retained references

### 1. Paul KI, Moser K. Unemployment impairs mental health: Meta-analyses. Journal of Vocational Behavior. 2009;74(3):264–282.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2009.01.001

Evidence reviewed: [Publisher abstract and DOI metadata](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001879109000037).

Supports: Unemployment–mental health association and longitudinal evidence, with health selection also relevant.

Limit: Does not isolate the effects of modern hiring processes.

### 2. Song Z, Uy MA, Zhang S, Shi K. Daily job search and psychological distress: Evidence from China. Human Relations. 2009;62(8):1171–1197.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1177/0018726709334883

Evidence reviewed: [Publisher abstract and DOI metadata](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0018726709334883).

Supports: Daily diary study of 100 unemployed searchers supported associations in both directions and a role for negative search experiences.

Limit: Observational, China; not a causal estimate for US platforms or an estimate of disorder incidence.

### 3. Phelan JC, Link BG, Tehranifar P. Social conditions as fundamental causes of health inequalities: Theory, evidence, and policy implications. Journal of Health and Social Behavior. 2010;51(suppl):S28–S40.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1177/0022146510383498

Evidence reviewed: [Indexed abstract and DOI metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20943581/).

Supports: Resources and social conditions as a theoretical framework for health inequalities.

Limit: Does not establish job seeking as an empirically demonstrated fundamental cause.

### 4. Price RH, Choi JN, Vinokur AD. Links in the chain of adversity following job loss: How financial strain and loss of personal control lead to depression, impaired functioning, and poor health. Journal of Occupational Health Psychology. 2002;7(4):302–312.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-8998.7.4.302

Evidence reviewed: [Indexed abstract, author-hosted article, DOI metadata](https://mprc.isr.umich.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Price_JOHP02.pdf).

Supports: Longitudinal links involving financial strain, personal control, and health after job loss.

Limit: Not an experiment on hiring-system features; does not eliminate mediation confounding.

### 5. Wanberg CR, van Hooft EAJ, Dossinger K, van Vianen AEM, Klehe UC. How strong is my safety net? Perceived unemployment insurance generosity and implications for job search, mental health, and reemployment. Journal of Applied Psychology. 2020;105(3):209–229.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000435

Evidence reviewed: [Indexed abstract and DOI metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31328926/).

Supports: Four-wave study in three countries associated perceived generosity with mental health through financial strain and time pressure.

Limit: Perceived generosity, not a randomized benefit policy; article issue year 2020, online year 2019.

### 6. Hausknecht JP, Day DV, Thomas SC. Applicant reactions to selection procedures: An updated model and meta-analysis. Personnel Psychology. 2004;57(3):639–683.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2004.00003.x

Evidence reviewed: [Author-institution abstract and DOI metadata](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/applicant-reactions-to-selection-procedures-an-updated-model-and-/).

Supports: Relations between perceptions of selection procedures, organizational attitudes, and self-perceptions.

Limit: Applicant satisfaction and self-perceptions are not psychiatric diagnoses.

### 7. Schinkel S, van Dierendonck D, van Vianen A, Ryan AM. Applicant reactions to rejection: Feedback, fairness, and attributional style effects. Journal of Personnel Psychology. 2011;10(4):146–156.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1027/1866-5888/a000047

Evidence reviewed: [Publisher abstract and DOI metadata; subtitle confirmed at publisher](https://doi.org/10.1027/1866-5888/a000047).

Supports: Performance feedback negatively affected rejected individuals’ well-being in one of two studies.

Limit: Does not imply that accurate status updates or all feedback are harmful; well-being is not a diagnosed disorder.

### 8. Bjørnshagen V. The mark of mental health problems. A field experiment on hiring discrimination before and during COVID-19. Social Science & Medicine. 2021;283:114181.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2021.114181

Evidence reviewed: [Indexed abstract, open article, DOI metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34216884/).

Supports: Norwegian correspondence experiment found reduced positive responses following disclosure of mental health history.

Limit: Norway and a particular disclosure manipulation; not US psychiatric incidence.

### 9. Vinokur AD, Schul Y, Vuori J, Price RH. Two years after a job loss: Long-term impact of the JOBS program on reemployment and mental health. Journal of Occupational Health Psychology. 2000;5(1):32–47.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-8998.5.1.32

Evidence reviewed: [Indexed abstract, author-hosted article, DOI metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10658883/).

Supports: Randomized workshop study of 1,801 participants with employment and mental health benefits at two years.

Limit: Workshop intervention, not a trial of job-board design. PubMed also gives an equivalent double-slash DOI alias.

### 10. National Center for Health Statistics. 2022 National Health Interview Survey questionnaires, datasets, and documentation. Sample Adult public-use data, codebook, and survey description. Accessed September 29, 2026.

Identifier: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhis/documentation/2022-nhis.html

Evidence reviewed: [Official CSV, relevant codebook entries, survey description](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Dataset_Documentation/NHIS/2022/adult-codebook.pdf).

Supports: Population, variables, response coding, weights, and design for 2022.

Limit: Employment category combines unemployment, layoff, and looking for work; not a direct hiring-exposure measure.

### 11. National Center for Health Statistics. 2025 National Health Interview Survey questionnaires, datasets, and documentation. Sample Adult public-use data, codebook, and survey description. Accessed September 29, 2026.

Identifier: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhis/documentation/2025-nhis.html

Evidence reviewed: [Official CSV, relevant codebook entries, survey description (including pages 35–36 on pooling and variance)](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Dataset_Documentation/NHIS/2025/srvydesc-508.pdf).

Supports: 2025 data availability, employment and symptom coding, public-use pooling guidance, response rate, fieldwork interruption.

Limit: NCHS warns that PSUs minus strata is a degrees-of-freedom approximation, not an exact NHIS result.

### 12. Kroenke K, Strine TW, Spitzer RL, Williams JBW, Berry JT, Mokdad AH. The PHQ-8 as a measure of current depression in the general population. Journal of Affective Disorders. 2009;114(1–3):163–173.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2008.06.026

Evidence reviewed: [Indexed abstract and DOI metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18752852/).

Supports: PHQ-8 symptom measure and threshold; issue year 2009, online year 2008.

Limit: Screening severity is not a clinician-confirmed diagnosis in this NHIS analysis.

### 13. Spitzer RL, Kroenke K, Williams JBW, Löwe B. A brief measure for assessing generalized anxiety disorder: The GAD-7. Archives of Internal Medicine. 2006;166(10):1092–1097.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.10.1092

Evidence reviewed: [Indexed abstract and DOI metadata; full page range and subtitle from PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16717171/).

Supports: GAD-7 symptom measure and severity threshold.

Limit: Screening severity is not a clinician-confirmed diagnosis in this NHIS analysis.

### 14. Zou G. A modified Poisson regression approach to prospective studies with binary data. American Journal of Epidemiology. 2004;159(7):702–706.

Identifier: https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwh090

Evidence reviewed: [Indexed abstract and DOI metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15033648/).

Supports: Log-link Poisson estimating equations with robust variance for binary outcomes.

Limit: The manuscript applies the approach to prevalence ratios and adds the NHIS complex survey variance; it does not use the naive Poisson variance.

### 15. Thomas JE. PsychologicalRecession. Public data, analysis code, manuscript, and audit records. GitHub repository. Revision dated September 29, 2026.

Identifier: https://github.com/jethomasphd/PsychologicalRecession

Evidence reviewed: [Repository inspected; revised files and results generated and checked locally](https://github.com/jethomasphd/PsychologicalRecession).

Supports: Reproducible implementation and complete audit trail.

Limit: Repository availability is checked after publication of the revision; no external peer review is implied.

## Original bibliography

All 64 original entries were screened. All 35 supplied DOI records were queried; 33 returned registry metadata and two returned 404. Metadata retrieval does not clear a citation for reuse. Non-DOI entries were not treated as verified simply because a recognizable organization or book was named. Unneeded or incompletely specified sources were removed rather than used to sustain numerical claims. The original manuscript is preserved without corrections in archive/.

### Material errors and unresolved records

- Original reference 18: Supplied DOI returned 404 in Crossref. Unverified; excluded. This is not proof that no related paper exists.

- Original reference 28: Registry title includes “A systematic review and thematic synthesis of qualitative evidence from Western economies.” Earlier online publication and later issue date need distinction. Excluded from the new argument.

- Original reference 33: DOI resolves, but original author list does not match. Registry authors are Frank, Mustard, Smith, Siddiqi, Cheng, Burdorf, and Rugulies. Excluded.

- Original reference 34: DOI resolves to “Contribution to the study on the ‘right to disconnect’ from work. Are France and Spain examples for other countries and EU law?” Original title is inaccurate. Excluded.

- Original reference 58: Supplied BMJ Open DOI returned 404. A related 2023 Thern article is in Journal of Epidemiology and Community Health 77:755–761, DOI 10.1136/jech-2023-220817, with a different author list. Do not silently equate records. Excluded.

- Original reference 62: Supplied DOI 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2024.105170 resolves to “Spillover effects of specialized high schools,” not a Finnish basic-income study. The associated policy claim was removed.
